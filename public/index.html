How to measure your bagWheels, handles and pockets count
  1. 1Length: the longest side. On a suitcase standing up, floor to the top of the handle, wheels included.
  2. 2Width: the middle side, across the front.
  3. 3Height: the shortest side: the depth, front to back, pockets included.
  • Measure the outside at the widest point, with the telescopic handle pushed all the way down. Wheels, carry handles, feet and outside pockets all count.
  • Measure it packed. A soft bag or an expandable zip can gain a few centimetres once it's full.
  • Mixed up length and width? No problem: we try every way the bag can turn, the same way it goes into the sizer at the gate.

What the colours mean
  • No extra cost. Your bag matches an allowance that is already included in the fare.
  • Fits, but costs extra. Your bag matches the cabin bag dimensions, but that cabin bag is not included in the base fare. You have to buy it.
  • Doesn't fit. Your bag is over the published limit on size, weight, or both.
  • Check the baggage rule. The operator describes what you can bring instead of publishing measurements — common on trains. We show their rule in their own words and never turn it into a yes or no.
  • Size not confirmed. The operator doesn't publish what we'd need to answer. A missing limit is not the same as no limit.

Handles and wheels change the answer

Some operators measure your bag with handles and wheels included; others measure the bag body and allow the wheels to stick out by a set number of centimetres. A bag labelled 55 cm can measure 58 cm in the sizer. Where an operator publishes which basis it uses, we say so on the result. Where it doesn't, we say that too.

Every limit is sourced and dated

Each result links to the operator's own page and shows the date a human opened that page and confirmed it. We don't copy limits from blogs or luggage brands, because that is how out-of-date measurements spread.

Fitting the rules is not a guarantee

Meeting a published limit is not a promise that staff will accept your bag at the gate. At least one operator states that paying at the gate does not guarantee a bag over the limit will be carried.
